# AI in Medical Documentation

`analysis-only` Phase II project. Legal foundations + practice-oriented guidelines for **AI-supported transcription, structuring, and improvement of medical documentation**, anchored on the [[mpassist]] use case. Stringent Swiss data-protection requirements blocked operational deployment of patient-data processing in a public hospital, so the project focused on systematic regulatory clarification — paralleling [[inspection-robots]] as a successful analysis-only outcome.

## Outcome — the regulatory roundtable as a knowledge-transfer format

The project's most reusable artefact is not a prototype but a **format**: a **regulatory roundtable** with 10+ start-ups in the AI-supported medical-documentation space, convened together with the competent data-protection supervisory authority. The booklet flags this as particularly effective because AI providers rarely have direct access to supervisory authorities. Sustained regulatory work with **Swissmedic**, the **Department of Health Canton Zürich**, and the **eHealth working group of swissICT**.

## Key learnings

- AI-supported medical documentation has both Large-Language-Model and operational dimensions; legal analysis must span data-protection + sectoral healthcare regulation simultaneously (per [[00-overview-phase2-build-and-share]] §04 Legal: "interdisciplinary legal issues are the norm").
- Regulatory roundtables are a high-leverage transfer format — bringing supervisory authorities and providers into direct exchange beyond bilateral case-by-case advice.
- The non-implementation of operational testing did not eliminate impact: recommendations were "quickly established as a reference within the healthcare system" (booklet §03).
