# Open-source AI is a viable procurement alternative when commercial doesn't fit

From [[00-overview-phase2-build-and-share]] §03 (Digital Eye Clinic, p. 11):

> *"Second, the project team built a modular AI diagnostic platform based on open-source technologies, as no commercial provider met the stringent requirements of a public hospital. In doing so, the project demonstrated the tangible potential of [[intrapreneurship]] and in-house development within the public healthcare sector."*

## Evidence base

- **[[digital-eye-clinic]]** — built an open-source modular AI diagnostic platform after surveying commercial providers and finding none that met public-hospital requirements (data protection, transparency, modifiability).
- **[[building-permits]]** — the working prototype's design favours **model-agnostic** integration and open-source-friendly architecture; this is partially driven by [[embag]] obligations on administrative software.

## How to apply

When evaluating commercial vs. open-source AI procurement, weigh:

1. **Data-protection fit** — can the commercial vendor process the data under your jurisdiction's regime? (Often a hard no in healthcare.)
2. **Transparency requirements** — can you audit what the system does? (Critical for administrative-law contexts; see [[fadp-art-21]].)
3. **Modifiability** — can you adapt the system as the use case evolves? (Critical for emerging or specialised domains.)
4. **EMBAG obligations** — if you're a Swiss public administration, [[embag]] presumes open-source disclosure of administrative software.

Open-source is not a default; it's a tool that wins under specific constraints. The [[digital-eye-clinic]] decision was forced, not preferred.
