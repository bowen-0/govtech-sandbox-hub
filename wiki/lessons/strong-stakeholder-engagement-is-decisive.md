# Strong stakeholder engagement is decisive

From [[00-overview-phase2-build-and-share]] §04 Organisation, p. 19:

> *"Successful projects are characterised by committed implementation partners who contribute time, resources, networks, and domain expertise (e.g. extensive testing in the smart parking project in Frauenfeld). Expectations towards implementation partners must be clearly defined from the outset."*

## The pattern across the corpus

Compare:

| Project | Partner engagement intensity | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| [[smart-parking]] (Frauenfeld) | Extensive — city resources, prolonged testing, post-pilot scaling | Production deployment, multi-canton roll-out |
| [[building-permits]] (Stadt Kloten) | Extensive — real applications, expert validation, multi-vendor coordination | Working prototype + 3,336-eval benchmark, postulate input |
| [[bridge-monitoring]] (SOB pivot) | Strong — physical access, train-traffic data, structural-engineering expertise | Successful real-world testing despite original-partner withdrawal |
| [[medical-documentation]] | Partner-data access blocked by regulation | Analysis-only outcome (still valuable) |
| [[inspection-robots]] | No suitable test partner identified | Analysis-only outcome |

The two analysis-only projects pivoted *not* because the model was wrong, but because partner engagement could not reach the required intensity.

## How to apply

When scoping a sandbox-style pilot:

1. **Pre-define partner expectations in writing** — time commitment, resource contribution, decision-making authority, data-sharing scope.
2. **Choose partners who can commit, not just who are interested.** Municipalities with operational autonomy tend to engage more deeply than larger institutions with diffuse approval processes.
3. **Identify partner-side champions** — individual contributors who personally own the engagement and have authority to unblock decisions.
4. **Plan engagement intensity at the budget level** — a partner relationship requiring ~10 hours/week of project-side investment is a different project shape than one requiring ~2.
