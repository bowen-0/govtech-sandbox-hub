# New ways of working with AI require active change management

From [[00-overview-phase2-build-and-share]] §04 Organisation, p. 19:

> *"AI reshapes existing processes and roles. Acceptance is fostered through transparent communication, training, and the involvement of employees (e.g. drone inspections as a complement to manual runway inspections)."*

## What it means in practice

AI doesn't replace people 1:1. It changes:

- **What people do all day** — manual runway inspectors now interpret drone-flagged anomalies rather than walking the runway themselves.
- **What skills matter** — pattern recognition, model calibration, exception handling.
- **Who is accountable** — when a drone misses a defect, whose error is it?

These shifts feel threatening if introduced without communication, training, and visible involvement.

## Evidence base

- **[[infrastructure-maintenance]]** — the booklet's explicit example: drone inspection as a *complement* to manual inspection, not a replacement. Acceptance hinged on framing the AI as augmentation.
- **[[digital-eye-clinic]]** — clinical AI is highly sensitive to clinician acceptance; the project's evaluation framework includes practitioner-experience dimensions.
- **[[building-permits]]** — administrative officials need to understand *what the AI checks* to trust its pre-checks.

## How to apply

When planning an AI deployment that touches a workforce:

1. **Frame the change in terms of the employee's value-add**, not in terms of automation savings.
2. **Involve operational staff in the design**, not just the leadership signing the procurement.
3. **Build training that goes beyond "how to use the UI"** to include "when not to trust the AI" — operational skepticism is a feature.
4. **Make the AI's behaviour observable** — staff trust what they can see.
