# Partner withdrawal → pivot is a normal sandbox execution pattern

The sandbox treats partner withdrawal as something to **work around**, not as a project failure. The Phase II corpus has three distinct responses to partner constraints:

| Project | Original plan | What happened | Pivot outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[bridge-monitoring]] | Municipal footbridge with original partner | Partner withdrew | Pivoted to SOB railway bridge in Wädenswil — successful real-world testing |
| [[inspection-robots]] | Operational testing of autonomous robots | No suitable test partner identified | Pivoted to pure regulatory analysis — analysis-only outcome |
| [[medical-documentation]] | Operational deployment in public hospital | Data protection blocked | Pivoted to legal analysis + regulatory roundtable — analysis-only outcome with policy impact |

## What makes the pivot work

From [[00-overview-phase2-build-and-share]] §03 (Dependencies on Test Partners, p. 15):

> *"Sandbox projects with a focus on practical implementation therefore require a strong openness to outcomes, as well as flexible and iterative prioritisation by the project team. Planning certainty can only be ensured to a limited extent; the key lies in the ability to identify dependencies at an early stage and to adjust timelines and project focus accordingly."*

## How to apply

When designing a sandbox or sandbox-like engagement:

1. **Identify the partner-dependency chain at intake**, not at execution.
2. **Pre-define acceptable pivot directions**: "if test-partner access fails, the project becomes a regulatory analysis."
3. **Treat analysis-only as a first-class outcome class**, not a failure mode — see [[real-world-testing]] for the booklet's framing of the distinction.
4. **Build the project plan around the partner risk**, not around the model risk.
