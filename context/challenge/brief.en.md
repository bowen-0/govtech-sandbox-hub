## Need / problem and target group

As part of the **AI Innovation Sandbox of the Canton of Zürich**, several AI pilot projects have been supported under real-world conditions over the past few years. The work produced reports containing valuable insights — for example on legal frameworks, data access, organisational prerequisites, technical challenges, and possible solution paths.

**A selection of sandbox projects:**

- **AI in building permits** — AI-based pre-check of building applications (e.g. solar installations, heat pumps).
- **Machine translation for administrative texts** — automatic translation of official documents.
- **Autonomous inspection systems** — AI-supported robots for facility and infrastructure inspections.
- **Automated infrastructure monitoring** — AI analyses sensor data for maintenance of structures.
- **Smart parking** — AI detects free parking spaces from camera images.
- **AI in medical documentation** — automatic generation of medical reports from spoken conversations.

Today, however, these findings are mainly documented inside individual reports. That makes access and practical use harder. Anyone planning a new AI initiative often has to laboriously search out relevant information, classify it, and transfer it to their own case.

The problem particularly affects:

- **Administrative staff** who review, support, or implement AI projects.
- **Companies and start-ups** developing or testing AI solutions in a regulated environment.
- **Other organisations** that want to learn from prior pilot projects.

What is therefore sought is a solution that prepares the existing knowledge in such a way that it becomes easier to use for these target groups, and applicable to new initiatives.

## (Not) available data

The central foundation is the existing reports and findings from the AI Innovation Sandbox. These contain concrete experience from already-implemented projects and form the substantive basis of the challenge.

**Expected to be available:**

- Selected reports from the AI Innovation Sandbox.
- Context on the goals and working methods of the sandbox.

**Not in focus:**

- Building a fully production-ready system.
- Extensive new data collection.
- A complete legal review of all content.
- Access to arbitrary additional administrative data outside the provided material.

The central question is therefore how the existing knowledge can be better opened up, structured, and made accessible.

## Expected benefit

If the problem is solved, future users will be able to access the knowledge from the sandbox faster and more precisely. Instead of working through long reports one by one, they get relevant pointers in a form that is understandable for their specific initiative and directly usable.

In particular, this would mean:

- Findings from earlier projects become easier to discover.
- Recurring challenges are recognised earlier.
- Important legal, organisational, and technical questions can be considered earlier.
- New AI projects can be prepared on a more solid footing.
- The knowledge built up in the sandbox has greater impact beyond individual projects.

A collection of reports would thereby become a practically usable body of knowledge for new AI initiatives.

## Goal for the hackathon

The goal of the hackathon is to develop a **convincing prototype or a clearly worked-out solution concept** that shows how the findings from the AI Innovation Sandbox reports can in future be made more accessible and usable.

The solution should ideally demonstrate how people with a concrete AI initiative can be guided to the findings most relevant to them — for example in the form of hints, classifications, checklists, or recommendations. It is important that the results remain **traceable** and can be traced back to the underlying reports. As a possible focus group, one could pick **administrative staff who want to start an AI project or pilot**.

## Solution approaches

One possible approach is to digitally prepare the existing reports and make them more usable with AI support.

The following elements are conceivable in particular:

- The contents of the reports are prepared so they can be searched systematically.
- Users describe a planned AI project or a question.
- The system identifies findings from the existing reports that fit the question.
- The solution presents the relevant hints in an understandable form — e.g. an overview, an answer, a checklist, or action recommendations.
- The statements are made traceable through references to the underlying reports.

In addition, it may be useful to capture content from the reports in a **structured** way — for example by topics such as data, regulation, governance, technology, or implementation. This improves search and makes it easier to recognise relevance for new use cases.

**What is already being pursued:** to support knowledge transfer, **short videos** have also been created for each report.

## Constraints

The challenge is to be tackled within a limited timeframe. Accordingly, this is **not** a fully finished end-solution, but a realistic and convincing prototype.

What to pay particular attention to:

- The solution should be understandable for **non-technical** users.
- Content must remain **traceable**; statements should be referable back to concrete report content.
- The solution should stick closely to the information that is actually present and **not generate unsubstantiated claims**.
- Handling of sensitive information and potentially legally relevant statements requires care.
- The focus is on **practical usefulness**, not on technical complexity for its own sake.
- A further challenge is the **currency / freshness** of the content — we have been producing reports for 4 years already, and the legal situation and assessment around AI can change quickly. On one hand it must be ensured that the information is correct (i.e. not outdated); on the other, it must be relatively straightforward to update information.

If possible and within the available time, **embedding on the AI Innovation Sandbox website** should also be considered.

## Sustainability

The results of the hackathon serve as a basis for further discussions on the future use and preparation of findings from the AI Innovation Sandbox.

The hackathon should therefore not only collect ideas, but also make visible **concrete approaches that have the potential to be pursued further, deepened, or adopted in adapted form** after the event.

## Supplement from the challenge owner's slides (`slides.pdf`)

The brief above is the public website text. The slide deck Lukas Willi (challenge owner, Projektleiter KI, Kanton Zürich) presents to teams adds framing that is **not** in the website brief and is materially load-bearing. Captured here so this English file is self-contained.

### The sandbox in one line (slide 2)

> *Innovation-Sandbox für KI — Testumgebung für konkrete KI-Anwendungsfälle im Kanton Zürich.*
>
> **AI Innovation Sandbox — a test environment for concrete AI use cases in the Canton of Zürich.**

The sandbox is presented as having **three pillars**:

1. **Clarify regulatory questions and develop best practices.**
2. **Provide data and enable partner access.**
3. **Share findings and give impulses for legislation.**

The hackathon challenge most directly serves pillar 3 — *sharing findings* — but the source reports are full of pillar-1 (regulatory) and pillar-2 (data/partner) content. A good prototype surfaces all three.

### All 10 projects, split by phase (slide 3)

> *Seit 2022 wurden in zwei Runden insgesamt 10 KI-Projekte abgeschlossen.*
> **Since 2022, 10 AI projects have been completed in two rounds.**

| Phase | Project | Subtitle on report cover |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase I (2022–24)** | Smart Parking | Best Practices für Bilderkennung |
| | Autonome Systeme | Ein Leitfaden für Regulierungsfragen |
| | Automatisierte Infrastrukturwartung | Drohneninspektionen mit Bilderkennung |
| | Maschinelle Übersetzung | Empfehlungen für die Verwaltung |
| | Künstliche Intelligenz in der Bildung | Rechtliche Best Practices |
| **Phase II (2024–26)** | Sensorbasierte Brückenüberwachung | Datenerfassung und Vorhersagen für den Infrastrukturunterhalt |
| | Digitale Augenklinik | Einführung von KI-Diagnostik in die klinische Praxis |
| | Autonome Inspektionsroboter | Umgang mit der KI-Verordnung und dem EU-Maschinenrecht |
| | KI in der medizinischen Dokumentation | Rechtsgrundlagen und Empfehlungen |
| | KI bei Baubewilligungen | Anwendungsfälle, Prototyp und Erkenntnisse für die Praxis |

Above these sits the **Play & Learn** umbrella report — *"Eine Sandbox zur Stärkung des KI-Standorts"* ("a sandbox for strengthening the AI hub"). This is the single best entry point for understanding the sandbox itself rather than any individual project.

The sandbox has received the **Digital Economy Award** — a credibility marker worth keeping in mind for the pitch.

Note that the website brief earlier in this document lists only **6 selected projects** out of these 10. The slides give the complete picture.

### The hackathon goal, in slide form (slide 4)

Slide 4 is titled *"Verbesserte Zugang zu Erfahrungen und Erkenntnissen aus Sandbox"* — **improved access to experiences and findings from the sandbox.** It splits the goal into two layers, and the layering is itself important:

> **Primary goal:** Structuring and preparing the data foundation
> *(e.g. Knowledge Graph, LLM-Wiki)*
>
> **Secondary goal:** Development of audience-specific applications on top of that data foundation
> *(e.g. Guideline-Generator, GraphRAG search engine)*
>
> **Available data:** All reports on the sandbox and the projects.

**Read this as a layering directive, not just a list.** The website brief leaves "what to build" open. The slides specify: get the **substrate** right first (graph / structured wiki / typed knowledge base with citations back to reports), then layer end-user apps on top. A prototype that optimises only for a flashy UI without a clean underlying data structure misses the explicitly-stated primary goal.

Slide 4 also restates the **problem** and the **target groups** more crisply than the brief:

**Problem (slide 4):**
- Results documented in **static reports**, one per project.
- Hindered access and practical use for the audiences below.

**Target groups (slide 4 — note the ordering):**
1. **Verwaltungsmitarbeitende** — administrative staff (primary audience).
2. **KI-Lösungsanbieter** — AI solution providers (companies, start-ups, vendors).
3. **Weitere Akteure aus Ökosystem** — other actors in the ecosystem (research, policy, NGOs).

The website brief (above) names the same three groups but in less compressed language. When citing the audience in the pitch, the slide-4 ordering is the authoritative one.
