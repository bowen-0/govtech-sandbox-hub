# Play & Learn — How to strengthen an AI hub with a sandbox

**Phase**: I (2022–24) — Overview report
**Topic**: Design and governance of a regulatory + practical AI sandbox
**Published**: September 2024
**Source**: reports/en/00-overview-phase1-play-and-learn.pdf
**Length**: ~31 pages

## Problem & context
Switzerland — and Zürich specifically — sits between a heavily regulating EU, a market-leading US, and a state-directed China, with no Swiss AI law and fragmented sectoral rules. Public administrations, SMEs and researchers were experimenting with AI in parallel, repeating the same legal clarifications and rarely sharing lessons. The sandbox was launched in 2021 to consolidate that knowledge and produce regulatory learning bottom-up rather than waiting for top-down legislation.

## Approach & methodology
A working group from 8 institutions (Office for Economy of Canton Zürich and Schwyz, Statistical Office ZH, Department for Digital Administration ZH, Zürich Metropolitan Area Association, ETH AI Center, ITSL University of Zürich, ZHAW entrepreneurship) ran a sector-independent programme combining two services — *regulatory consulting* and *data provisioning* — applied flexibly per project. 45 proposals were received across two calls (2022, 2024); 5 projects were implemented in Phase I via real-world testing rather than purely theoretical legal assessment. No financial remuneration; participants kept their IP but had to publish findings.

## Key findings & recommendations
- Practical implementation (vs. pure legal review) surfaces issues that theoretical analysis misses; e.g. installing 500 parking cameras revealed that the hardest problems were *non*-AI (power, property-owner coordination), not the algorithm.
- Sector-independent design produced wider innovation spread but required broader expertise — minimum team is programme manager, legal expert, data scientist, communications.
- Almost no proposals came from the Swiss financial sector (only 1 of 45) — likely because fintechs already have legal capacity in-house. Start-ups dominated submissions.
- Real launch took only **5 months** from formal start to first call. Iterative implementation per project lasted up to **1.5 years**.
- The sandbox deliberately did NOT seek new legal instruments (no experimentation clauses, no no-action letters); it operated entirely within existing law. Pragmatic trade-off: speed vs. depth of regulatory support.
- Two of seven initiated projects failed to reach implementation (CO2 emission forecasting; semantic search in legal documents) because data-owner partners pulled out — planning must allow for this.
- The Council of States adopted a postulate for an "innovative environment for AI testing and promotion" drawing directly on this programme — sandbox outputs influenced national policy.

## Legal & regulatory points
- EU AI Act art. 3(55) definition of "AI regulatory sandbox" introduced in 2024 — formal anchor that the Zürich programme predates.
- Experimentation clauses and no-action letters discussed as instruments NOT available in Swiss law in relevant areas; programme chose to work within existing data protection, copyright and sector-specific frameworks instead.
- Cantonal data protection law (IDG Zürich) referenced as the operating legal frame for cantonal public bodies; FADP for federal/private actors.

## Data, models, infrastructure
No central technical infrastructure — data and tools are provided per project by partners. International benchmarking with UK and France sandboxes informed design. Knowledge transfer artefacts: 5 PDF best-practice reports, blog posts, the AI Sandbox Summit (Jan 2024, Zürich, 7 European sandbox operators), workshops, scientific papers. An international sandbox-projects database is under consideration.
